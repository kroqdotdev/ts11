# Languages: English and Danish

teamsheet11 comes in English and Danish. Danish has to read as if a Danish club volunteer
wrote it, not as a translation. This file is the guide for anyone writing either language.

## How it works

- **Interface text** lives in `messages/<lang>/<area>.json` and is read with next-intl
  (`useTranslations` in client components, `getTranslations` on the server). English is the
  reference; Danish must have exactly the same keys (checked by the type in
  `messages/index.ts` and by `tests/unit/messages.test.ts`).
- **URLs:** English at the root (`/studio`, `/account`), Danish under `/da` with Danish paths
  (`/da/studio`, `/da/konto`, `/da/privatliv`, `/da/nulstil-adgangskode`). See
  `src/i18n/routing.ts`. Link with `Link`, `useRouter` and `redirect` from
  `@/i18n/navigation`, never from `next/link` or `next/navigation`, so links stay in the
  reader's language. A Danish browser is sent to `/da` on its first visit; the language
  switch (`LocaleSwitch`) is remembered after that.
- **Graphics** print in the club's own language (`team.language`), not the interface's: a
  Danish volunteer can make English graphics and the other way round. Printed words live in
  `src/lib/graphicText.ts`. A new club gets the interface language.
- **Emails** go out in the language of the page that asked for them.
- **Dates and numbers** go through `Intl` with `en-GB` or `da-DK` (`INTL_LOCALE` in
  `src/i18n/routing.ts`), or next-intl's `useFormatter`. Never build them by hand.

## Writing Danish

**Voice.** Plain, dry and friendly, the way a helpful person at the club would put it. Use
_du_, never _De_. Short sentences. No exclamation marks, except on a goal. No officialese:
never _venligst_, _Bemærk venligst_, _såfremt_, _endvidere_, _foretage_, _herunder_,
_hermed_. Never _Klik her_.

**Rewrite, don't translate.** Start from what the English means and say that the way a Dane
would. English idioms don't survive translation: "You're all set" is _Så er du klar_, not
_Du er helt sat op_. "Nothing here yet" is _Her er ikke noget endnu_. When English plays on
words, write something plainly good in Danish instead.

**Compounds are written together.** _klublogo_, _spillernavn_, _trøjenummer_,
_baggrundsbillede_, _e-mailadresse_, _kampdag_. Splitting them (_klub logo_) is the most
common sign of translated Danish. Join an abbreviation with a hyphen: _PNG-fil_, _MB-grænse_.

**Case.** Sentence case everywhere: buttons, headings, menu items (Danish never uses title
case). After a colon, carry on in lowercase unless a full sentence follows.

**Buttons** are short verbs in the imperative: _Gem_, _Slet_, _Hent PNG_, _Opret konto_,
_Log ind_, _Videre_, _Tilbage_, _Annuller_ (cancel), _Fortryd_ (undo). The same action keeps the same word all the way
through: the button that says _Slet_ gives the toast _Slettet_.

**Punctuation and numbers.**

- Decimal comma and thousands point: _2,5 MB_, _1.000_.
- Time with a point: _kl. 15.00_. Dates: _lør. 21. nov._ or _lørdag den 21. november_
  (that is what `Intl` with `da-DK` gives).
- Quote names of buttons and things with »…«: _Tryk på »Hent PNG«_.
- Dash: a spaced en dash ( – ), never an em dash.
- Genitive: _Ashby Vales logo_; after s, x or z only an apostrophe: _Lars' mål_.
- Plurals with ICU: `{count, plural, one {# spiller} other {# spillere}}`.

**Loanwords Danes actually use** stay: _e-mail_, _upload_ (_uploade_, _uploadet_), _link_,
_app_, _backup_, _PNG_, _studio_, _login_ (as a noun; the verb is _log ind_). Avoid
anglicisms where Danish has a normal word: _gem_ not _save_, _hent_ not _download_ on
buttons, _indstillinger_ not _settings_.

**Names stay.** teamsheet11 and the look names that are proper names (e.g. a font) are not
translated.
